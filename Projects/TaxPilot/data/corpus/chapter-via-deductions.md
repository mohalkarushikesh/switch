# Chapter VI-A Deductions (Old Regime)
> source: Income-tax Act, 1961 | rules: 80C, 80CCD1B, 80D, 80TTA, 80TTB, 80G

These deductions are available under the old regime only. They reduce gross total
income to arrive at total (taxable) income, and together cannot exceed gross total
income.

## Section 80C
Investments and payments such as life-insurance premium, Employees' Provident
Fund, Public Provident Fund, ELSS mutual funds, principal repayment of a home
loan, five-year tax-saving deposits, and children's tuition fees qualify under
Section 80C (together with 80CCC and 80CCD(1)), subject to an overall ceiling of
Rs 1,50,000.

## Section 80CCD(1B)
An additional deduction of up to Rs 50,000 is allowed for contributions to the
National Pension System (NPS), over and above the Rs 1,50,000 limit of Section
80C.

## Section 80D - health insurance
Health-insurance premium for self and family is deductible up to Rs 25,000, or up
to Rs 50,000 where the insured is a senior citizen. A further deduction is
available for premium paid for parents.

## Sections 80TTA and 80TTB - interest
Section 80TTA allows a deduction of up to Rs 10,000 on interest from a savings
account for taxpayers below 60. A senior citizen instead claims Section 80TTB, a
deduction of up to Rs 50,000 on interest from deposits.

## Section 80G - donations
Donations to specified funds and charitable institutions are deductible, either at
50% or 100% and some subject to a qualifying limit of 10% of adjusted gross total
income. A valid donation receipt with the institution's registration is required.
